Follow one correction.
Keep every cent explainable.

An API request arrives late. Another is corrected. The original statement must remain reproducible, the next delivery must be complete, and finance must understand exactly why its balance changed.

Synthetic local exercise · 15 November 2026. Real loopback HTTP and restricted PostgreSQL 17.11; 14 checks passed across 14 requests. No customer traffic, collected money or hosted backend. This page serves immutable evidence.

01 · CAPTURE

Use the actual API ledger

Two scoped accounts, effective-dated prices and three immutable statement revisions. Bearer credentials never enter the export.

02 · EXPLAIN

Preserve the units

Integer microUSD becomes invoice cents under an explicit rounding policy. Every residual stays visible.

03 · RECONCILE

Deliver a complete version

Hashes, revision counts and predecessor identity distinguish exact replay from missing or changed delivery.

The changed statement

Customer A's late correction removes 10,001 units at 250 microUSD each. Another late event adds 4,007 units at that rate. Customer B stays unchanged.

What the run established

  • Retrying an event and statement produced byte-identical export files.
  • The next bundle retained both original statements and added the corrected revision.
  • A's bearer could not export B's account.
  • An injected interruption after staging preserved the prior complete bundle.

Boundaries worth stating

This is a bounded statement export, not a live CDC system. Each account has its own source cutoff; the two captures are not a cross-account transaction. Checksums detect changes but do not authenticate a producer. Atomic directory visibility does not establish power-loss durability. Invoice rounding and downstream collection inputs are clearly labelled simulations.
